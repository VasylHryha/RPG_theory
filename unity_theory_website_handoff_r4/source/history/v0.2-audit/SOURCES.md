# RRG research audit — source register

Checked 30 September 2026. This is a targeted literature audit, not an exhaustive novelty search or a systematic review. A paper showing one mechanism is not evidence that the full RRG conjecture follows. Full text was used where available; abstract-level checks are identified below. Bibliographic URLs identify the inspected source, not an endorsement of every claim in it.

## S01 — Coupled geometry and oscillator phase

Kevin P. O’Keeffe, Hyunsuk Hong and Steven H. Strogatz. **Oscillators that sync and swarm.** Nature Communications 8, 1504 (2017). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-017-01190-3.

Source: `https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-01190-3`

Checked: primary model, equations and collective states in full text. Relevant result: specified phase–position coupling produces distinct collective organizations. Limitation: the interaction functions are model inputs, not a derivation of fundamental forces.

## S02 — Wave/geometry backreaction

Markus Aspelmeyer, Tobias J. Kippenberg and Florian Marquardt. **Cavity optomechanics.** Reviews of Modern Physics 86, 1391 (2014). DOI: 10.1103/RevModPhys.86.1391.

Source: `https://arxiv.org/html/1303.0733v1`

Checked: scholarly review's model and optomechanical interaction sections. Cavity displacement changes optical frequency; radiation acts mechanically on the cavity. This supplies a physical setting for the audit's independently worked, explicitly simplified adiabatic example.

## S03 — Organization in reaction systems

Peter Dittrich and Pietro Speroni di Fenizio. **Chemical organization theory.** Author manuscript, arXiv:q-bio/0501016 (2005); subsequently published in Bulletin of Mathematical Biology.

Source: `https://arxiv.org/html/q-bio/0501016v1`

Checked: definitions, Dynamic Analysis, Theorem 1 and its limitations. Under the stated kinetic support assumptions, the species present at a fixed point constitute a closed, mass-maintaining organization. The converse does not follow; an organization is not automatically dynamically stable.

## S04 — Importance of kinetic details

Kaleta et al. **Using chemical organization theory for model checking.** Bioinformatics 25(15), 1915–1922 (2009).

Source: `https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/25/15/1915/212126`

Checked: publisher abstract/search extract. Useful as an additional warning that kinetic details, not just a reaction-network sketch, matter. No detailed theorem from this paper is relied on in the calculations.

## S05 — Nuclear networks analyzed with organization theory

Pedro Maldonado-Lang and Clément Vidal. **Emergent Complexity in Nuclear Reaction Networks: A Study of Stellar Nucleosynthesis through Chemical Organization Theory.** Author manuscript arXiv:2601.06143 (January 2026). A proceedings version is listed under DOI: 10.1162/ISAL.a.914.

Sources: `https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06143v1` and `https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06143`

Checked: full author manuscript's methods and stated results. Applies organization analysis to a STARLIB-based nuclear reaction network across temperatures. This is computational network analysis, not a new observation of stars, a derivation of nuclear forces, or evidence for life inside stars. Those stronger interpretations are not used here.

## S06 — Experimental molecular replication

Tracey A. Lincoln and Gerald F. Joyce. **Self-sustained replication of an RNA enzyme.** Science 323, 1229–1232 (2009). DOI: 10.1126/science.1167856.

Sources: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19131595/` and `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2652413/`

Checked: primary-paper abstract and available indexed paper content. Cross-catalytic RNA systems assemble copies from supplied substrates. This is not a demonstration that any stable molecular shape copies itself or a complete reconstruction of life's historical origin.

## S07 — Spatial patterns need not be temporal oscillations

A. M. Turing. **The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis.** Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 237, 37–72 (1952). DOI: 10.1098/rstb.1952.0012.

Source: `https://www.dna.caltech.edu/courses/cs191/paperscs191/turing.pdf`

Checked: primary PDF, including discussion of stationary versus oscillatory cases around printed page 51. Relevant limit: spatially patterned chemical dynamics are not necessarily sustained temporal oscillations.

## S08 — Exact block reduction of networks

Florian Dörfler and Francesco Bullo. **Kron Reduction of Graphs With Applications to Electrical Networks.** IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I 60(1), 150–163 (2013). DOI: 10.1109/TCSI.2012.2215780. Author preprint arXiv:1102.2950.

Sources: `https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2950` and `https://motion.me.ucsb.edu/pdf/2011d-db.pdf`

Checked: primary abstract and available paper description of Laplacian Schur-complement reduction. The audit derives the dynamic, frequency-dependent block identity explicitly rather than attributing every dynamic assertion to this static-network result.

## S09 — Coarse-graining can retain memory

Thomas Hudson and Xingjie H. Li. **Coarse-Graining of Overdamped Langevin Dynamics via the Mori–Zwanzig Formalism.** Multiscale Modeling & Simulation. DOI: 10.1137/18M1222533. Author preprint arXiv:1810.08175 (2018).

Sources: `https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/18M1222533` and `https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08175`

Checked: primary publisher abstract and author preprint description. Derives reduced evolution and demonstrates the importance of memory for temporal behavior. The short block-ODE derivation in this audit is worked independently.

## S10 — Spectral geometry, with substantial physical input

Ali H. Chamseddine and Alain Connes. **The Spectral Action Principle.** Author manuscript arXiv:hep-th/9606001 (1996).

Source: `https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9606001`

Checked: full text, especially introduction equations (1.16)–(1.24). The algebra, fermionic representation and Yukawa data supply substantial Standard Model information. This qualifies earlier RRG statements that sounded as though generic frequency plus geometry had already derived the known particles.

## S11 — Scale-dependent effective theories

Nicolas Dupuis et al. **The nonperturbative functional renormalization group and its applications.** Physics Reports 910, 1–114 (2021). DOI: 10.1016/j.physrep.2021.01.001. arXiv:2006.04853.

Source: `https://arxiv.org/html/2006.04853v3`

Checked: review sections on the effective average action, flow, effective potential and approximations. RG supplies a transformation of a specified theory with resolution; it does not supply an unknown microscopic theory, nor is its scale variable physical cosmic time.

## S12 — Emergent gauge-like behavior in specified models

Michael A. Levin and Xiao-Gang Wen. **Photons and electrons as emergent phenomena.** Author manuscript arXiv:cond-mat/0407140 (2004).

Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0407140`

Checked: primary abstract. Certain microscopic constructions admit emergent gauge/particle-like descriptions. No claim is made that arbitrary resonator networks necessarily do so, or that the model reproduces all observed fundamental physics.

## S13 — Neural geometry and learning

Patrick T. Sadtler et al. **Neural constraints on learning.** Nature 512, 423–426 (2014). DOI: 10.1038/nature13665.

Source: `https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13665`

Checked: primary abstract and experiment description. Rhesus-macaque brain–computer-interface learning over hours was easier for within-manifold than outside-manifold mappings. This is evidence about neural population constraints, not a proof of a universal theory of mind.

## S14 — Physical computation can be trained

Logan G. Wright et al. **Deep physical neural networks trained with backpropagation.** Nature 601, 549–555 (2022). DOI: 10.1038/s41586-021-04223-6.

Source: `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791835/`

Checked: full paper's experiments and limitations. Mechanical, electronic and optical systems were trained for computational tasks using physics-aware training. Training and interfacing still use digital machinery; the experiments do not establish automatic energy or intelligence superiority for RRG.

## S15 — Thermodynamics of open reaction systems

Riccardo Rao and Massimiliano Esposito. **Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics of Chemical Reaction Networks: Wisdom from Stochastic Thermodynamics.** Physical Review X 6, 041064 (2016). DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevX.6.041064. arXiv:1602.07257.

Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07257`

Checked: primary abstract. Gives energy, entropy and nonequilibrium free-energy balances for elementary mass-action reaction networks, including time-dependent chemostats. It provides an appropriate thermodynamic starting point for a chemical RRG model, rather than treating persistence as energy-free.

## S16 — Observed interaction framework

CERN. **The Standard Model.** Official scientific overview.

Source: `https://home.cern/science/physics/standard-model/`

Checked: institutional account of interactions and their scope. Used for the distinction between four interactions and four exclusive size bands; not used as evidence for RRG's unification conjecture.

## Boundaries of this audit

This source set is enough to correct the present model and define a concrete next test. It is not enough to claim that RRG is novel, that all previous citations in the v0.1 documents are verified, or that every technical claim in the full cosmology-to-life conversation has been checked. Unverified 2026 references from earlier drafts are not carried into the evidence base merely because they appeared in the chat.
