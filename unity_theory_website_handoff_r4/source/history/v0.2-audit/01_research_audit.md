# RRG v0.2 — Research audit and revised purpose

**Date:** 30 September 2026  
**Status:** literature-grounded research hypothesis, with two worked restricted mathematical examples.  
**Relationship to earlier files:** this audit qualifies and corrects v0.1b; it does not silently overwrite it.

## 1. The idea being evaluated

RRG proposes that spatial organization and temporal organization belong to the same physical system; persistent combinations can become effective components of further combinations.

Here, **geometry** means arrangement, connectivity, order, orientation and constraints. **Mode structure** preserves the user's broad meaning of frequency: spectra, phases, amplitudes, collective patterns, response and timescales. It does not mean one universal Hertz value.

The central intuition remains:

> Lower structures remain active inside a higher structure. Their relationships constrain the collective behavior, and the collective organization constrains their relationships.

The research question is no longer simply whether this pattern occurs. It is how to identify an effective higher-level unit, predict its response, and say precisely when this representation succeeds or fails.

## 2. Three claims that must not be merged

**Organizational claim.** Persistent systems can be represented at multiple scales, with internal structure encoded in effective behavior. Many scientific methods already establish versions of this under explicit conditions.

**Mechanistic claim.** Geometry–mode feedback selects or maintains particular structures. This is demonstrated in particular model families and physical settings, not for every form of persistence.

**Fundamental-unification claim.** All particle species, quantum laws, spacetime and four interactions follow from one deeper resonant-geometric rule. Neither our documents nor the sources audited here establish that claim.

Proving the first does not automatically prove the second or third. Conversely, useful progress on the first two does not require first solving the third.

## 3. What the research actually supports

### Physical geometry and timing can jointly organize a system

Swarmalator models explicitly couple positions and oscillator phases. Cavity optomechanics supplies another route: moving a boundary changes a field frequency, and the field acts back on the boundary. These are concrete settings for two-way coupling, rather than a metaphor. Their force laws and material parameters are supplied to the models. [S01, S02]

**RRG consequence:** keep geometry–mode feedback as an identifiable mechanism. Do not promote one example into a derivation of every interaction.

### Chemistry already has a close organization framework

Chemical Organization Theory studies closed, mass-maintaining sets in reaction networks. Under specified kinetic assumptions, the species present at a fixed point form an organization. The reverse implication is not guaranteed: a structurally admissible organization need not be dynamically stable. [S03]

**RRG consequence:** this is useful mathematics for the parts-maintain-whole loop. Reaction structure, rates, resources and perturbation behavior must all be retained. We need not invent chemical closure from scratch.

### The nuclear-to-chemical bridge already has direct prior art

Maldonado-Lang and Vidal apply Chemical Organization Theory to stellar nuclear reaction networks, using STARLIB data and temperature-dependent analysis. This is strikingly close to the proposed nuclear-organization portion of RRG. It is network modeling, not a derivation of nuclear forces or the origin of life. [S05]

**RRG consequence:** remove earlier suggestions that no existing work connects these domains. A novelty claim would require comparison with this and other prior work.

### Replication is physical, but persistence is not sufficient

Lincoln and Joyce demonstrated cross-catalytic RNA replication using supplied substrates. Existing molecular organization can therefore participate in making further instances through specific chemistry. [S06]

For RRG, recurrence, templating and biological reproduction may remain under the same abstract umbrella. However, their production terms differ. Calling all three propagation does not supply the missing catalytic pathway, materials or energy budget.

### Structure does not have to be visibly oscillating to be stable

Turing's morphogenesis analysis distinguishes stationary spatial patterns from oscillatory cases. [S07] A simpler mathematical example is \(\dot x=-kx\), \(k>0\): disturbances return to equilibrium without oscillating.

With RRG's broad temporal meaning, these remain legitimate dynamical systems. But the narrower statement “every stable structure requires an active periodic resonance” must be removed. A mode can describe relaxation as well as oscillation.

### Lower-level activity survives coarse-graining as memory

Mori–Zwanzig reduction shows why eliminating internal coordinates can produce history-dependent effective dynamics. Hudson and Li explicitly demonstrate the importance of memory for correct temporal behavior. [S09]

**RRG consequence:** a higher-level component should not automatically be one mean phase or one simple oscillator. It can need multiple modes, internal state and a memory kernel. This preserves the user's insistence that smaller dynamics continue inside the larger structure.

### Neural and artificial computation provide a limited but useful bridge

Sadtler and colleagues found that rhesus-macaque BCI learning over hours was easier for activity mappings within the existing neural manifold. [S13] Wright and colleagues trained physical mechanical, electronic and optical transformations to perform computational tasks. [S14]

**RRG consequence:** structure-sensitive learning and physical computation are real research directions. Neither experiment establishes that the whole mind is one resonance, or that RRG automatically outperforms digital AI.

## 4. Corrections required in the old mathematics

### Self-consistency is not stability

Both \(\dot x=-x\) and \(\dot x=+x\) admit the self-consistent solution \(x=0\). Only the first attracts nearby states. A repeating solution can likewise be unstable. Thus a closed geometry–mode loop is a candidate state, not a completed stability proof.

### An action is not generally a potential energy

The old draft wrote \(F=-\partial\Gamma/\partial q\) while calling \(\Gamma\) an effective action. That is not a general force law. An action is a functional of a trajectory or field history. A gradient force follows from an appropriate **potential energy**, and in suitable equilibrium reductions from a **free energy**. A stationary action also need not be a minimum.

For small conservative mechanical disturbances, use a potential \(U\), a mass matrix \(M\), and a stiffness matrix \(K=\nabla^2U\):

\[
K u=\omega^2 M u.
\]

For dissipative dynamics, examine the linearized generator and its growth/decay rates. For periodic states, examine perturbations over a cycle.

### Graphs do not manufacture extra microscopic degrees of freedom

Collective coordinates organize existing degrees of freedom into useful modes. Effective constraints may reduce the freedoms that need tracking at a larger scale. New *effective descriptions* are not creation of independent microscopic variables from nothing.

### A mean phase can erase a real collective pattern

If two parts have phases \(\theta\) and \(\theta+\pi\), then their mean complex amplitude is zero. They can nevertheless form a perfectly organized antiphase pattern. The v0.1 mean-phase promotion rule therefore fails as a universal detector of higher units.

### Renormalization is not physical aging

RG transforms descriptions with resolution; the cosmic environment evolves in physical time. These can be related within a specified model, but cannot be equated. RG also requires a microscopic theory and approximation choices; writing its general flow equation does not determine an unknown theory. [S11]

## 5. Reassessing the four forces

Three ideas were being mixed: an existing interaction can dominate a different regime; its effective coupling can run with scale; or a collective system can admit a genuinely emergent interaction description. These are different claims. The four interactions are not four exclusive size bands. [S16, S11]

Specific models can exhibit emergent gauge-like behavior. [S12] But that does not derive the observed charges, particle content or gravity from unspecified resonators.

The spectral-action paper is also more conditional than earlier responses implied. It uses a chosen algebra, a Hilbert space based on known fermions, and Yukawa information. It does not infer the Standard Model from an arbitrary frequency spectrum alone. [S10]

**Revised status:** fundamental unification remains a separate hypothesis. A successful coarse-graining or self-assembly result would support the organizational research program, not by itself prove that hypothesis.

## 6. A more precise higher-level unit

Proposed working representation:

\[
R_n=(G_n,z_n,\chi_n;B_n).
\]

Here \(G_n\) records relevant constraints and interfaces; \(z_n\) is retained internal state; \(\chi_n\) is the response to disturbances, including modes and memory when needed; and \(B_n\) specifies the environment.

This is a proposed representation, not a claimed newly discovered law. It keeps the full temporal side operational rather than burying it in the word “frequency.”

The useful test is:

> Can this representation predict the larger system's observable behavior over a declared range of perturbations, environments and times, with fewer resources than tracking every part?

That introduces an error tolerance and a cost criterion. It does not assume that every grouping is a meaningful new object.

## 7. What was calculated in this audit

Two restricted examples were worked rather than only proposed.

**Geometry–mode feedback:** an ideal slow cavity coordinate coupled to a fixed-action wave has an explicitly calculable equilibrium, field frequency and restoring stiffness. The example shows a self-consistent change of geometry and frequency, conditional on a supplied mechanical potential and wave dispersion relation.

**Repeated response reduction:** an eight-site damped spring network was reduced from 8 to 4 to 2 retained coordinates. The nested result matched direct 8-to-2 reduction at numerical roundoff, and its boundary response matched the full network. The same check was repeated for 16 perturbed networks.

The exact reduction retains a frequency-dependent response. It does not generally become another two-mass constant-spring system. This distinction is why the old “same small equation at every scale” requirement was too strong.

Neither example demonstrates spontaneous assembly, replication, biological evolution or all fundamental forces. The network's connections and retained coordinates were deliberately specified. The exact algebra is standard, not a novelty claim.

## 8. What has actually been closed

**Closed within a stated model:** geometry–mode feedback can be written and solved; repeated linear-response reduction can preserve measurable response exactly.

**Existing conditional theory available:** reaction-network organizational closure; reduced dynamics with memory; energy and entropy balances for driven chemical networks. [S03, S09, S15]

**Not established by these results:** spontaneous discovery of effective units; compact nonlinear closure across unrelated systems; a common new law beyond existing theories; fundamental-force unification; an RRG AI advantage.

We should no longer label all of these “the same blocker.” They need different tests.

## 9. Revised research objective

> **RRG investigates when interacting structures admit persistent, predictive higher-level descriptions that retain the constraints and temporal response relevant to further interaction. It tests whether a common procedure for finding these descriptions transfers across physical, chemical and computational systems.**

This is narrower than a Theory of Everything and more useful than an unrestricted statement that everything has structure and changes.

A successful result would be a demonstrated transfer: a method designed and fixed on one system identifies effective organizations and predicts responses in a different system, without using that system's answer to choose the representation.

Fundamental unification can remain a longer-term extension. It should not be used as the premise of the first experiment.
