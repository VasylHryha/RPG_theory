## 1. The name states the rules

| Word | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Resonant** | resonance | Motion falls into rhythm. |
| **Geometry** | shape | Rhythm and shape hold each other, so the thing lasts. |
| **Recursive** | resonator, and the repeating cycle | A lasting resonator spreads and joins into bigger shapes with a new resonance. The same rules repeat at each new scale. |

Rhythm and shape hold each other; neither comes first (core §3). The
explanation starts with motion only because everything moves.

## 2. Motion starts as noise; order appears by chance

Chaotic motion is like noise, with every rhythm mixed together and none standing
out. Shapes and rhythms appear in it by chance, and most vanish. Now and then
one holds: its shape and its rhythm keep each other going. It has become a
resonator. If it lasts long enough, it starts to change and order its
surroundings, and that order spreads. Nothing chooses it. Chance creates the
opportunity, and what holds is what remains. (04 §§3–4, 18)

**In plain words:**

> Everything moves. Most motion is chaos. Sometimes a piece of motion falls into
> a rhythm that keeps its own shape, and that shape keeps the rhythm going. It
> lasts: that is a resonator. Its rhythm reaches out. It makes nearby things move
> with it, stick to it, or take a similar shape. So more resonators appear, they
> join into bigger shapes with a new rhythm of their own, and the same thing
> happens again at the next level.

## 3. Energy and time

Everything moves, and energy is what keeps things moving: vibrating, rotating,
resonating. Every resonator lasts for a time. Some hold on their own for billions
of years; others last only while energy keeps flowing through them. Stability is
lasting for an interval, not permanence (core §4).

## 4. Spreading

A resonator's rhythm reaches out and tends to make more of the same, when it can
couple to its surroundings and has energy. The forms of spreading are:
- the same thing appearing again where conditions repeat;
- catalysis;
- copying.

Life's reproduction is the elaborate late form. Spreading is a strong tendency,
not a required step at every level. (01 §9; core §9; 06 E10, E11, E20, E01, E02)

## 5. Changing the surroundings

A resonator changes its surroundings. This can make similar things more likely,
or make new things possible (or impossible). (04 §§4, 11, 13; core §11 treats
this as an extension compatible with the core)

A lasting thing therefore has two separate roles:
- it can become a building block;
- it can change the conditions.

## 6. Forces gather and hold; resonance decides what fits and lasts

At each scale, a different force does most of the holding:
- the strong force in nuclei;
- electromagnetism in atoms, chemistry and life;
- gravity in stars, planets and galaxies.

Resonance decides which arrangements fit together and stay stable. Inner
resonance holds a thing together as itself; outer resonance links it to others.

*Hypothesis status:* whether some of the four forces are themselves a form of
resonance is open (core §10). (06 E18, E19, E01, E07)

## 7. Each level needs the ones before it

The chain runs:
1. hydrogen and helium;
2. the first stars forge and scatter new elements;
3. a new star (our Sun) and planets form from that richer material;
4. water and steady sunlight;
5. conditions for life.

Life is likely impossible without every earlier step, just as stars were
impossible before the hydrogen epoch. Breaking down is part of the cycle: dying
stars scatter the elements that later build planets and life. (01 §7, ladder
addendum; 04 §16)

## 8. New levels bring new ways to interact

New levels bring new ways to interact: the nuclear force appears with nuclei,
and chemical bonds appear with atoms.

*Speculative hypothesis:* the fundamental forces may also have appeared as the
universe's scale and time changed, created by earlier levels. This may never be
directly confirmed. (04 §§15, 29; core §10)

## 9. Evolution follows the same rules

Surviving comes first, but surviving is not enough. The most successful life
acts as a true resonator: it shapes its environment for itself and spreads as far
as it can. It adapts to almost any place; some spores even survive exposure to
space. It behaves as if it tries to dominate, though no intention is needed: what
holds and spreads becomes more common.

Joining is another way to secure itself: cells became bodies, and organisms
became ecosystems. The frontier grows more complex, while simpler levels continue
underneath. (04 §§10, 17, 30)

## 10. Humans, and perhaps AI, are the newest cycle

Humans are the most striking example of life as a true resonator. We reshape our
world for ourselves with tools, cities and even waves we create and control, and
we have spread across the planet. AI may be the beginning of a new cycle; this is
an open, speculative question. (01 §§12–13; 04 §31; ladder addendum)

## 11. How RRG should be presented

- **What it is:** a unifying framework, not yet proven, updated over time, with a
  few sharp hypotheses.
- **Keep history and reading apart:** established science is kept visibly
  separate from RRG's reading of it.
- **Where to test it:** RRG can be checked where we can reach:
  - life changing its environment is confirmed;
  - simple experiments show resonance shaping matter, rhythms synchronizing and
    patterns multiplying;
  - the strongest test is the full step happening twice in one system
    (04 §34, Experiments 5–6).
- **Beyond direct evidence:** only the extremes, the origin of forces and the
  very beginning, may stay beyond direct evidence.
