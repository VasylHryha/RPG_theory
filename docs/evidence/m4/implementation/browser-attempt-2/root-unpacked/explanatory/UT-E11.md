## 2. Emergent magnetic charges and Coulomb-like interactions in spin ice

Spin-ice materials contain microscopic magnetic moments constrained by lattice geometry and frustration.

Collective correlations in this many-body geometry permit excitations that behave as **emergent magnetic monopoles** rather than elementary magnetic charges.

Castelnovo, Moessner & Sondhi predicted these quasiparticles as emergent manifestations of the correlated spin-ice system.

Bramwell and collaborators later experimentally measured effective magnetic charges and currents in Dy$_2$Ti$_2$O$_7$, showing that these emergent charges interact through a Coulomb potential.


$$
\boxed{
\text{microscopic spins}
+
\text{collective geometric constraints}
\rightarrow
\text{emergent quasiparticles}
+
\text{Coulomb-like effective interaction}
}
$$


### RRG relevance

This demonstrates that a collective geometry can generate:

- new effective particle-like degrees of freedom;
- a higher-level interaction law;
- behavior that is not apparent from treating each microscopic spin independently.
