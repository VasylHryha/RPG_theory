## 1. Phonon-mediated effective attraction in superconductivity

In conventional superconductivity, electrons couple to lattice vibrations (phonons).

At the microscopic level, electrons and ions interact electromagnetically. The organized crystal lattice, however, possesses collective vibrational modes. Electron–phonon coupling can generate an **effective electron–electron attraction** in the appropriate regime, allowing Cooper pairing and superconducting order.


$$
\boxed{
\text{crystal geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{phonon modes}
\rightarrow
\text{effective electron interaction}
\rightarrow
\text{Cooper pairing / superconducting order}
}
$$


The effective attraction does not replace electromagnetism as a fundamental interaction. It is a higher-level interaction generated through collective lattice dynamics.

## 2. Emergent magnetic charges and Coulomb-like interactions in spin ice

Spin-ice materials contain microscopic magnetic moments constrained by lattice geometry and frustration.

Collective correlations in this many-body geometry permit excitations that behave as **emergent magnetic monopoles** rather than elementary magnetic charges.

Castelnovo, Moessner & Sondhi predicted these quasiparticles as emergent manifestations of the correlated spin-ice system.

Bramwell and collaborators later experimentally measured effective magnetic charges and currents in Dy$_2$Ti$_2$O$_7$, showing that these emergent charges interact through a Coulomb potential.


$$
\boxed{
\text{microscopic spins}
+
\text{collective geometric constraints}
\rightarrow
\text{emergent quasiparticles}
+
\text{Coulomb-like effective interaction}
}
$$


### RRG relevance

This demonstrates that a collective geometry can generate:

- new effective particle-like degrees of freedom;
- a higher-level interaction law;
- behavior that is not apparent from treating each microscopic spin independently.

### Evidence

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06433

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08500

## 3. Background-field state changes the observable interaction regime

Electromagnetic and weak interactions are described within electroweak theory.

As the early Universe cooled, the Higgs field settled into a non-zero vacuum configuration. The $W$ and $Z$ bosons acquired mass while the photon remained massless.

That changes low-energy interaction behavior:

- massive $W/Z$ carriers imply a very short-range weak interaction;
- the massless photon supports long-range electromagnetism.


$$
\boxed{
\text{background field state}
\rightarrow
\text{particle spectrum}
\rightarrow
\text{effective interaction ranges / behavior}
}
$$
