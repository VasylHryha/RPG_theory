## Start with parts and relationships

Think of a stretched string. Its length, material, tension and fixed ends constrain how it can move. Change those conditions and the possible motions change too. Shape alone does not explain the result: material properties and physical interactions matter.

Recursive Resonant Geometry, or **RRG**, uses “geometry” for a system's organization at a chosen scale. That includes its components, connections, relationships, boundaries and constraints. It means more than a visible outline.

## Add what happens over time

The second aspect is **mode structure**: the organized ways a system can change, respond or move. This includes characteristic times, phases, coupling and collective activity. A single number in hertz does not describe all of it.

RRG treats organization and mode structure as two aspects of one system. Organization constrains activity; activity can support, maintain, transform or destroy organization. Neither is universally first.

This is the framework's proposed way of describing a system. Whether a particular model makes useful predictions requires separate tests.

## A whole can become a part

A molecule can participate in a larger arrangement. A cell can participate in a tissue. In the RRG framework, compatible lower-level systems may form a higher-level organization while retaining their internal activity. The components can differ and play different roles.

“Recursive” describes that possibility of organized wholes participating in further organization. It does not mean every system must copy itself, or that every scale must obey the same microscopic equation.

## Existing organization can change the possibilities

The question also concerns the environment: an existing organization may change the conditions under which further organization becomes possible. This is an extension to investigate, rather than a guarantee that complexity must increase.

The current research also asks when lower-level organization generates or enables an **effective interaction**—an interaction useful in a higher-level description—that could help another organized state persist. The evidence documents discuss specific physical systems. Those examples do not derive all four fundamental forces from RRG.

## What the examples do—and what remains open

These examples introduce relationships the project investigates. Different systems have different mechanisms and conditions; an analogy does not demonstrate a universal law.

The current sources distinguish core definitions from extensions, candidate mathematical models and evidence in particular systems. Open work includes a useful cross-domain criterion and novel predictions beyond existing frameworks. The technical website sections will make those distinctions visible alongside their sources.
