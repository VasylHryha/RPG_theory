## What happens

Long before ordinary atoms existed, the early universe contained a hot mixture of quarks and gluons, called a quark–gluon plasma. As it cooled, quarks became bound into particles such as protons and neutrons.

Protons and neutrons became ingredients for light nuclei. Early nuclear reactions produced mostly hydrogen and helium nuclei, with traces of other light nuclei. Further cooling allowed neutral hydrogen and helium atoms to form. The early chemical story also includes helium hydride, HeH⁺, the first known type of molecule, which could form before the first stars.

There are now lasting pieces that can take part in later organization. The next step can use what already holds together.

## How RRG reads it

RRG proposes a common pattern beneath these transitions. In fluctuating activity, temporary shapes and rhythms appear by chance. Most vanish. Sometimes an arrangement and its activity keep each other going. RRG calls that lasting pair a [resonator](/glossary/#resonator).

**Resonant** names the organized activity; **Geometry** names the arrangement that supports it. Rhythm and shape hold each other; neither comes first. Here “rhythm” includes quantum activity. A [mode](/glossary/#mode) describes activity, relationships and response, without requiring a visible beat.

## What you can see here

The main outward role is [building block](/glossary/#building-block). Protons and neutrons participate in nuclei; atoms become available for chemistry. The lower pieces keep their internal activity when a larger whole forms.

This opens the **Recursive** question: can a lasting whole become a part of another lasting whole? Becoming a part and changing the surrounding conditions are separate roles. Neither guarantees that another level will form.

## Where the interpretation stops

RRG’s reading of particle formation is a proposal, not derived physics. Its noise picture supplies no measured account of the primordial activity. The established history needs its physical interactions and conditions. The idea that fundamental forces themselves arose through earlier levels is speculative; no sequence of force origins has been demonstrated here.
