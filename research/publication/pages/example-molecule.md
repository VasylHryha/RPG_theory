## What happens

Atoms form a molecule, but an inventory of atoms does not yet describe it. We also need to know which atoms are bonded, how they are arranged and how they move together.

Bonds restrict distances and angles. Electron states reorganize, and the molecule has collective vibrations, rotation and deformation. The atoms retain internal activity while participating in the whole.

## How RRG reads it

RRG calls the arrangement and constraints [geometry](/glossary/#geometry-shape). The whole’s organized activity belongs to its [mode structure](/glossary/#mode). Parts help form the whole, and the whole constrains its parts.

**Resonant** concerns the organized activity; **Geometry** concerns the arrangement that supports it. Where they hold together, RRG reads the whole as a [resonator](/glossary/#resonator). Its [inner resonance](/glossary/#inner-resonance) means compatible internal activity, rather than an extra force holding the atoms.

## What you can see here

The main outward role is [building block](/glossary/#building-block). An intact molecule can participate as one unit in a larger chemical arrangement while its internal structure remains active. That opens the **Recursive** question: can a lasting whole become a part of another lasting whole?

Treating the molecule as one unit is useful while unresolved internal details do not change the answer to the question being asked. If it breaks apart, the intact-unit description no longer fits. This role is separate from physically changing the surroundings; a simpler description alone creates no new material organization.

## Where the interpretation stops

Molecular bonding needs quantum chemistry, its interaction laws, energy and conditions. Atoms are not tiny classical solar systems. The shared RRG description identifies an organizational relationship; it does not replace the mechanisms of bonding or demonstrate an entire repeated ladder.
