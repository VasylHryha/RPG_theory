## What happens

Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon, but their atoms are arranged differently. In diamond, each atom bonds to four neighbours in a rigid three-dimensional network, so it is very hard. In graphite, the atoms form layers, so it is soft and slippery and conducts electricity. [OpenStax explains the contrast](/references/#BIB-0093).

The same distinction matters for molecules. Keep the kinds and numbers of atoms the same, but change which atoms connect or how they sit in three-dimensional space. The resulting molecules can behave differently.

An inventory tells us what is present. The connections and arrangement tell us how those ingredients form this particular whole.

## How RRG reads it

RRG calls these relationships [geometry](/glossary/#geometry-shape). The word includes how parts connect and constrain one another, beyond the visible outline. A different arrangement changes which patterns of activity the whole can support. RRG calls this organized activity and response its [mode structure](/glossary/#mode).

**Geometry** concerns the arrangement; **Resonant** concerns the activity it supports. In RRG’s reading, the identity of a whole depends on its components, their relationships and their activity together. The parts remain active inside the whole.

## What you can see here

The main lesson is **variation**: the same lower pieces can participate in different higher wholes. Reusing the ingredients does not require repeating the arrangement or the behaviour. The [core’s variation definition](/claims/UT-D07/) makes room for those differences without adding another rule.

This shows the [building-block](/glossary/#building-block) role. Atoms participate in a larger organization, and the relationships between them matter to what that organization becomes. The **Recursive** question then follows: which of those wholes can remain intact and participate in something larger?

Changing the surroundings is a separate role. This example follows arrangement within a whole; it does not by itself show that the whole creates conditions for another one to form.

## Where the interpretation stops

This is RRG’s organizational reading of a chemical distinction. Predicting a particular material’s properties still needs its actual arrangement, interactions and conditions. The diamond–graphite contrast illustrates the source’s general point; it is not a quantitative RRG prediction.
