## Start with something you can see

Watch coupled metronomes settle into a shared rhythm, or sand form a pattern on a vibrating plate. Then ask how a lasting pattern changes what can happen around it.

RRG proposes that formation, spreading and changed surroundings can join into a repeating cycle. Forces gather and hold; [resonance-fit](/glossary/#resonance-fit) is the proposed rule for which arrangements fit together and last. A [resonator](/glossary/#resonator) is a lasting pair of shape and activity; its [background](/glossary/#background) is the material, activity and conditions around it.

## At home or in a classroom

Before each step, record the conditions, what you measure and the expected result. These demonstrations check familiar effects; failure first calls for checking the setup, and challenges an RRG model only where it made a clear prediction.

### Step 0 — rhythms coordinate

**Do:** place metronomes on a shared movable support and record their timing. **Shows:** mechanically coupled rhythms can coordinate. **Rule:** coupling between existing oscillators, part of Resonant. **What would count against it:** the predicted coordination fails under the stated conditions.

The [metronome study](/evidence/catalogue/#e01--experimental-study-of-the-triplet-synchronization-of-coupled-nonidentical-mechanical-metronomes) found triplet coordination even when pairs were asynchronous. Coordination need not mean identical timing or frequency. The metronomes, power and coupling were supplied; this checks an existing effect rather than creating oscillators.

### Step 1 — activity arranges matter

**Do:** observe sand on a vibrating plate; vary frequency, vibration strength or boundaries and record the pattern. **Shows:** driven activity can rearrange suitable material. **Rule:** the activity-to-shape side of Geometry. **What would count against it:** a predicted pattern change is absent under the stated conditions.

The [Chladni study](/evidence/catalogue/#e02--controlling-the-motion-of-multiple-objects-on-a-chladni-plate) moved particles through controlled vibration sequences. Sand patterns are a simpler observation, without the study's camera and control program. Neither demonstrates independent self-maintenance or reproduction.

## In a research lab

Steps 2–6 need suitable apparatus, a defined system and a measurement plan; their success would still need comparison with other explanations.

### Step 2 — shape changes activity

**Do:** alter the resulting arrangement or boundaries and measure the supported [modes](/glossary/#mode)—patterns of activity and response—again. **Shows:** whether shape changes activity. **Rule:** the reverse side of Geometry, completing the two-way loop. **What would count against it:** the predicted activity change is absent, leaving only one-way forcing.

### Step 3 — a lasting pair forms

**Do:** start with a fluctuating active medium, without supplying the final shape, and look for a lasting shape–activity pair. **Shows:** formation from the supplied background. **Rule:** Resonant and Geometry together. **What would count against it:** lasting organization requires the final shape to be inserted, or the predicted pair never lasts.

### Step 4 — it changes the surroundings

**Do:** compare the frequencies present, relationships between activity at different places, or the surroundings' response before and after formation. **Shows:** a measurable background change. **Rule:** the background-changing part of Recursive. **What would count against it:** the predicted change is absent or entirely caused by an external change in driving.

### Steps 5–6 — the key test: the full step twice

**Step 5 — Do:** compare which lasting arrangements can form in the original and changed backgrounds. **Shows:** whether a new kind becomes possible or much easier to form. **Rule:** changed background enabling further organization. **What would count against it:** no new kind appears, or it was equally possible before.

**Step 6 — Do:** repeat formation and background change in the same system, keeping the same kind of governing equations. **Shows:** whether the operation repeats. **Rule:** the stronger mathematical version of Recursive. **What would count against it:** a second step needs manually added rules or fails where predicted.

Keeping the same kind of equations is stronger than observing the cycle repeat; the broad proposal allows different microscopic mechanisms. Spreading needs a separate observation before claiming the full cycle.
